## Physical layout (pipes, sections, dimensions, materials, cavity, openings)

### Takeaway
The organ has 35 pipe systems in 7 stepped segments of 5 pipes each, along a stepped seawall about 70–75 m long. Each pipe system has three parts:

1. A large horizontal polyethylene (PE) inlet pipe under water (D ≈ 0.3 m).
2. A sloped PE riser of 50–125 mm diameter, inclined about 20–55°.
3. A short horizontal stainless-steel "acoustical" pipe under the walkway. It is stopped at the far end and has a labium (flue mouth) at its entry.

Sound leaves through holes in the top step and through slits in the vertical faces of the steps.

### Cited Findings
- **Count and grouping.** The structure is divided into seven segments, and each segment contains five organ pipes of different lengths and diameters, for 35 in total. — [Krvavica et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314).   - Popular sources give 70 m: "seventy meters long". — [OddMusic](http://www.oddmusic.com/gallery/om24550.html); [EnterCroatia](https://entercroatia.com/cities/zadar/organ-of-the-sea/)
  - ArchDaily says the pipes together span 70 m. — [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/777512/hear-this-croatian-seawall-sing-as-the-wind-and-waves-lap-the-shore?video=true)

### Inferences
- **"35 pipes along 70 m"** describes the whole installation, not the length of the pipes. Each acoustic pipe is short and horizontal; the long parts are the sloped PE risers, about 2–5 m.
- **Section footprint.** At 5 pipes × 1.5 m spacing, each section has about 6 m of active pipes. 7 sections over about 70–75 m leaves gaps between sections, roughly 10 m pitch per section.
- **L3 = 0.3 m looks wrong for the low notes.** Krvavica's L3 is a modelling placeholder for hydraulics, not an acoustic length. A stopped pipe sounds at a quarter wavelength, L ≈ c/4f, which gives these lengths (before end correction):

  | Note | Frequency | Required stopped-pipe length |
  |---|---|---|
  | C2 | 65 Hz | ≈1.31 m |
  | G2 | 98 Hz | ≈0.87 m |
  | B3 | 247 Hz | ≈0.35 m |

  The real resonators are likely longer for the low notes, or include part of the gallery or cavity.

### Gaps
- No public, verified per-pipe table of resonator lengths and diameters. Krvavica et al. state that the exact dimensions are not public.
- The geometry of the labium (mouth width, cut-up, flue-slit size) is undocumented beyond the 112 mm² orifice estimate used in the model.
- The size and volume of the "resonating cavity" or gallery are not found.

## Tuning (notes, chords, pitch ranges, who tuned)

### Takeaway
The 7 segments alternate between two chords, five pipes and five tones per segment:

- Odd segments 1, 3, 5, 7 play G major: D–G–d–g–h.
- Even segments 2, 4, 6 play C major with an added sixth (C6): C–D–c–e–a.

That is 9 distinct pitch classes/octave positions in total, all in the great and small octaves (about 65–250 Hz). Ivica Stamać designed the scheme and the Heferer workshop built and tuned the pipes. The harmony is said to follow the "matrix" of Dalmatian klapa singing.

### Cited Findings
- "Every odd segment is specifically tuned to produce five tones from a G-major chord (D-G-d-g-h), whereas every even segment produces five tones from C-major chord with additional sixth (C-D-c-e-a)... All tones correspond to frequencies in the range 60-250 Hz." — [Krvavica et al., citing Stamać 2005, 2007](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- The AES 2007 abstract says the pipes are tuned to nine tones of the G and C6 major chords. — [AES e-lib 14132 (abstract via search)](https://aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=14132)
- Stamać 2005: the "7 successive groups of pipes" are "alternately tuned to two musically cognate chords of the diatonic major scale". In the same paper: "The Grand and Small musical octaves (frequencies between 65 Hz and 250 Hz) were chosen for allocation of tones to the pipes. The flue pipes emanate their noblest sonority in these octaves." — [croatia.org repost (search excerpt)](http://www.croatia.org/crown/articles/9359/1/nikola-baiae-author-of-the-zadar-sea-organ.html)
- Stamać "composed seven sound clusters, each of two major chords (G and C6), that alternate steps", and "the choice of tones and chords was made on the musical matrix of the klapa song". — [Architectuul](https://architectuul.com/architecture/the-sea-organ)
- "Each five-pipe section is tuned to one musical chord", and a listener at any point hears about 5–7 tuned pipes. — [OddMusic](http://www.oddmusic.com/gallery/om24550.html)
- **Popular-press errors:**
  - Some sources describe the output as "seven chords of five tones", implying seven different chords. That is wrong: there are only two chords, alternated.
  - A claim that chords "change in real time with wave height" is unsupported.
  - A claim that the tuning is "random" is also unsupported. What is random is which pipes sound, not the tuning.
  - Search summaries: [storyhunt](https://www.storyhunt.io/en/articles/zadar-sea-organ), [youtubemusicsucks](https://youtubemusicsucks.com/sea-organ-of-zadar-croatia-unique-musical-instruments/)

### Inferences
The note names use German/Helmholtz convention: h = B, capital letters = great octave, lower case = small octave. Assuming 12-TET at A4 = 440 Hz (the reference pitch and temperament are not documented):

**Odd segments (G major), 5 pipes:**

| Note | Name | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| D | D2 | 73.42 Hz |
| G | G2 | 98.00 Hz |
| d | D3 | 146.83 Hz |
| g | G3 | 196.00 Hz |
| h | B3 | 246.94 Hz |

**Even segments (C6), 5 pipes:**

| Note | Name | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| C | C2 | 65.41 Hz |
| D | D2 | 73.42 Hz |
| c | C3 | 130.81 Hz |
| e | E3 | 164.81 Hz |
| a | A3 | 220.00 Hz |

- The union of the two sets is 9 distinct pitches: C2, D2, G2, C3, D3, E3, G3, A3, B3. This matches the "nine tones" in the AES abstract and the 65–250 Hz range exactly, which is a good consistency check on the inferred mapping.
- Collectively the pitch classes are C D E G A B, so the cluster sounds pentatonic or hexatonic and "diatonic major". G major and C6 share G, C and D in their pitch content, which explains the consonant overlap at section boundaries.
- D2 appears in every section, so it acts as a common drone.
- The order of the 5 pipes within a section (low to high along the promenade, or interleaved) is not documented.

### Gaps
- No documented measured frequency list, temperament, or reference pitch.
- Not confirmed whether the Heferer tuning is equal-tempered or just.

## Sound-producing mechanism

### Takeaway
The sound is a wave-driven oscillating-water-column (OWC) air pump feeding stopped flue (labial) pipes:

1. Sea-surface motion forces water up and down the sloped PE pipe.
2. The water compresses the air trapped above it.
3. The air is pushed through a labium orifice (edge tone) into a stopped stainless resonator.

Nature sets when each note starts and stops and how loud it is. The geometry sets the pitch.

### Cited Findings
- Stamać deliberately chose "stopped flue pipes (pipes with mouths – labiums and with tuning stoppers)" for their "rounded" timbre. — [croatia.org Stamać 2005 excerpt (via search)](http://www.croatia.org/crown/articles/9359/1/nikola-baiae-author-of-the-zadar-sea-organ.html). The AES 2007 abstract also says "35 stopped flue pipes". — [AES](https://aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=14132)
- "Each organ pipe is blown by a column of air, pushed in turn by a column of wave-moved water, through a plastic tube immersed into the water." — [Stamać AES abstract, via schoolofthefuture.eu](https://www.schoolofthefuture.eu/en/sea-organ)
- Krvavica et al.: "the vertical movement of the sea surface in front of the sea organ forces the water level oscillations inside the pipes; the internal water mass then compresses the air pushing it through the acoustical pipe, where a sound of a predefined frequency is finally produced... nature itself determines the duration and intensity of each note, but the arrangement of the pipes... governs the resulting melody." The excitation comes from waves, tides and passing boats. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- "The dimension of the labium orifice governs the air velocity, it may also affect the water level and air pressure oscillations." Air compressibility was found to be significant enough to affect the internal water oscillations. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- **Water-column dynamics.** The water column behaves as a damped single-degree-of-freedom oscillator, with fn = (1/2π)·√[g·sinφ / (L1·A2/A1 + l2)]. Krvavica et al. Table 2:

| Segment | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fn (Hz) | 0.218 | 0.266 | 0.311 | 0.277 | 0.327 | 0.457 | 0.538 |
| fd (Hz) | 0.216 | 0.265 | 0.310 | 0.276 | 0.325 | 0.453 | 0.533 |
| ζ (damping ratio) | 0.115 | 0.101 | 0.103 | 0.087 | 0.116 | 0.137 | 0.140 |
| fr (Hz) | 0.215 | 0.263 | 0.308 | 0.275 | 0.323 | 0.449 | 0.528 |

  The response is strongest near resonance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
  - Note: the paper prints Eq. 33 with a 2π prefactor, but the physically correct form is 1/(2π)·√(k/m). Use the tabulated values (Table 2), which depend on the equilibrium water-column length l2, rather than recomputing.
- **Simulated pressure amplitudes.** Two wave cases were simulated:
  - Case 1: Hs = 0.4 m, light northwest wind, peak frequency 0.34 Hz.
  - Case 2: Hs = 1.0 m, strong southeast (jugo) wind, peak frequency 0.22 Hz.

  Figure 9 axes show mean air-pressure-drop amplitudes on the order of hundreds of Pa (axis to about 800 Pa) for Case 1 and up to about 2–2.5 kPa for Case 2. Water-level amplitudes are on the order of tens of cm. The middle segments give the loudest sound. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- **Segment behaviour.** In Case 1, segments 3 and 5 have the highest pressure amplitudes and segments 1 and 6 the lowest. In Case 2, segment 4 is highest and the last two are lowest. This matches the authors' own experience on site: "the sound from the first segment is quieter than the others or even non-existing during small waves, but for higher wave heights, sound from this segment can be heard quite loudly." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- **Scale-model calibration.** The model was calibrated in a 12.5 m wave flume:
  - Pipes: D1 = 32 mm, D2 = 26 mm.
  - Labium cap orifices: 6, 12 and 18 mm². The best fit was A0 = 12 mm².
  - Waves: 0.3–0.7 Hz.
  - Fit errors: RMSE of 5.1 mm on water level and 45.8 Pa on pressure.

  — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- **Companion paper.** Krvavica, Ružić and Ožanić, "Integrated computational model for Sea Organ simulation", *Građevinar* 70(4), 2018, pp. 287–295, DOI 10.14256/JCE.2171.2017. It couples random waves, a 1D water oscillation model and a conceptual sound model (MIDI). It found that the sound depends on pipe geometry and on wave length and direction. — [Hrčak](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/201353); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4e06d34ff32a4159b128200cf201c7dd)

### Inferences (for DSP)
- **Two time scales.**
  - A slow control envelope at about 0.2–0.55 Hz, i.e. 2–5 s periods: wave-driven pressure, one "breath" per wave. Each segment behaves as its own band-pass resonator at fr with ζ ≈ 0.09–0.14 (Q ≈ 3.5–5.5), driven by the sea-surface spectrum.
  - Fast audio oscillators at 65–247 Hz.
- **Two-way breathing.** Because the water column oscillates, air is pushed out on the rising phase. On the falling phase air may be drawn back in through the labium, and the flue may sound weakly or not at all. Expect phrases shaped like inhale and exhale sighs. This is my inference from the OWC model; one popular source says the motion "pushes or pulls air".
- **Stopped flue pipe spectrum.** A stopped flue pipe gives mostly odd harmonics (1, 3, 5...) with a strong fundamental. That is the "rounded", flute- or gedackt-like timbre Stamać wanted. Wind noise and turbulence at the mouth add a breathy component, especially at onset and at low pressure.
- **Pressure dependence.** In flue pipes, pitch rises slightly with blowing pressure, and overblowing to the 3rd harmonic (stopped pipes skip the 2nd) happens at excess pressure. Here the pressure sweeps from 0 to kPa levels on every wave, so expect:
  - pitch scoops or bends at onsets;
  - a noisy, under-blown start;
  - possible momentary jumps to a 12th above at storm pressures.

  These follow general organ-pipe physics, not Zadar measurements. Treat them as modelling assumptions.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of pitch versus pressure, overblowing threshold, attack transient, or flue noise for the Zadar pipes.
- Whether the labium sounds on inflow (suction) as well is undocumented.
- The tuning-stopper material and how often the pipes are retuned are unknown.

## Measured or analyzed acoustic properties (spectra, loudness, polyphony, calm vs. rough sea, character)

### Takeaway
I found no published spectral analysis, SPL measurement, or noise-to-tone ratio for the Zadar organ. The only quantitative acoustic facts are the design pitches (65–250 Hz, nine tones) and modelled air-pressure amplitudes. Qualitative descriptions consistently report low, sighing, whale-like, klapa-chordal tones that swell and fade with the waves. The sound gets louder and more segments join in as wave height rises.

### Cited Findings
- **Stamać's recordings.** The 2007 AES paper included an "audio recording of organ sounds caught at the location in various weather conditions" played during the session. A spectral breakdown was not found. — [AES Vienna 2007 P25](https://www.aes.org/events/122/papers/session.cfm?code=P25) (via search snippet)
- **Kapusta 2007.** Krvavica et al. also cite "Kapusta, 2007" as analysing the acoustics. I did not locate this reference. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- **Polyphony.** "A listener, standing or sitting on a chosen point on the scalinade, should be able to hear 5 to 7 musically tuned pipes play their natural music." Which pipes sound and in what combination depends on how the wave energy is randomly distributed. — [OddMusic](http://www.oddmusic.com/gallery/om24550.html)
- **Calm versus rough sea.**
  - Higher waves give stronger pressure (hundreds of Pa at Hs 0.4 m, up to about 2 kPa at Hs 1.0 m).
  - Segment 1 is nearly silent in small waves but loud in big ones.
  - Each segment's loudness depends on how well the wave peak period matches that segment's resonance (0.2–0.55 Hz) and on local wave reflection, since the crest heights differ.

  — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- **Timbre descriptions.**
  - "Somewhat random but harmonic sounds". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_organ)
  - "Random but fairly harmoniously arranged tones". — [Architectuul](https://architectuul.com/architecture/the-sea-organ)
  - Some listeners compare it to whale song. — [absolute-croatia / search summary](https://www.absolute-croatia.com/zadar/attractions/sea-organ)
  - A traveller called it "low, rhythmic, and haunting". — [unpluggedtraveler](https://unpluggedtraveler.substack.com/p/the-sea-organ-of-zadar)
  - Passing boats' wakes also excite it. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)

### Inferences
- **Beating and interference.** Within a section, the five tones form a consonant chord with octave doublings (D2/D3, G2/G3 in G sections; C2/C3 in C6 sections). Beating will come mainly from:
  - temperament or mistuning;
  - pressure-dependent pitch drift between pipes blown at different pressures;
  - the overlap of G and C6 sections. G major and C6 together create a B–C semitone, between B3 and C3 in different octaves, and an A–G whole-tone colour.

  No measurements were found.
- **Loudness.** No SPL data. Kilopascal-level blowing pressures exceed typical organ wind pressures (about 0.5–1.5 kPa), so storm conditions could overblow the pipes. This is an inference only.

### Gaps
- No spectra, SPL, noise-to-tone ratio or recorded frequency analysis in accessible literature. The full texts of Stamać 2005/2007 and Kapusta 2007 may contain them but were inaccessible (paywall or bot block).
- The "sighing" or "breathy" quality is described only qualitatively.

## Spatial characteristics (radiation from openings, listener position)

### Takeaway
Sound radiates from many small distributed apertures: holes along the top step's walkway surface and slits in the vertical step faces. The sound sources are spread across roughly 70–75 m, with five 1.5 m-spaced sources per section. A listener mostly hears the 5–7 nearest pipes, so walking along the promenade crossfades between G-major and C6 sections.

### Cited Findings
- Each section's 5 pipes are spaced 1.5 m apart. A listener at any point hears 5–7 tuned pipes. The openings are on the walkway, with apertures in the vertical faces of the upper steps. — [OddMusic](http://www.oddmusic.com/gallery/om24550.html)
- "The sound emanates from the top and side openings in the steps." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314). Wikipedia shows the holes along the top step. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_organ)
- A single unverified report says the sound is aimed out to sea and inaudible from inside the city. — [ArchDaily (search summary)](https://www.archdaily.com/777512/hear-this-croatian-seawall-sing-as-the-wind-and-waves-lap-the-shore?video=true)

### Inferences
- **For an emulation.** Model 7 spatially separated groups of 5 point or line sources. Use alternating chords, distance attenuation, and the different wave timing per section.
  - Waves reach the segments at different times because they arrive obliquely. Krvavica et al. note that wave direction matters, which produces travelling or rippling chord entries along the wall.
  - Add broadband sea-wash noise and splashes as the bed.
- The cavity under the steps probably adds coloration, but this is undocumented.

### Gaps
- No directivity or sound-field measurements found.
- No data on audibility distance.

### Brief contrast with other wave organs
- **San Francisco Wave Organ (1986).** Untuned PVC and concrete pipes produce stochastic gurgling sounds from wave and tide action, with no designed harmony. — [Krvavica et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09314)
- **Blackpool High Tide Organ (2002).** Not researched here, outside the scope of this task.
