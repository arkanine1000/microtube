# Beat Bands

The band label at the top of Studio names the speed of the beat. These labels
borrow from brainwave vocabulary, but in MicroTube they are best understood as
listening regions.

## Delta

Delta is below 4 Hz. The pulse is slow. It can feel heavy, spacious, or sleepy.
At very low beat speeds, the wobble may be less obvious and more like a slow
sway.

Use Delta when you want the least busy motion.

## Theta

Theta runs from 4 to 8 Hz. It often feels dreamier than Alpha and less heavy
than Delta. The Schumann resonance preset at 7.83 Hz sits near the border
between Theta and Alpha.

Use Theta when you want a slow pulse that still has a clear rhythm.

## Alpha

Alpha runs from 8 to 13 Hz. This is the default neighborhood for relaxed
attention. A 10 Hz beat is quick enough to be present but not so fast that it
becomes buzzy.

Use Alpha as your neutral reference point.

## Beta

Beta runs from 13 to 30 Hz. The beat becomes faster and more energetic. Some
listeners experience Beta settings as sharper or more work-focused.

Use Beta when Alpha feels too slow.

## Gamma

Gamma begins around 30 Hz. At this speed the beat can become a fine shimmer
more than an obvious pulse. Gamma settings often benefit from lower volume and
less extra texture.

Use Gamma carefully and keep the sound comfortable.

## Labels are not promises

The labels do not force the brain into a state. They tell you where the beat
sits on a common map. Think of them like tempo markings: slow, walking,
moderate, quick, very quick.

MicroTube gives you a controlled sound. Your body, headphones, attention, and
environment decide how that sound lands.
