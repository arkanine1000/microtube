# First Listen

MicroTube is easiest to understand if you begin with what reaches your ears.
It sends one steady tone to the left ear and a slightly different steady tone
to the right ear. Your headphones keep those tones separate. Your brain hears
the difference between them as a slow pulse called the beat.

If the left ear receives 220 Hz and the right ear receives 230 Hz, the beat is
10 Hz. You do not need to memorize the math. The important idea is simpler:
the Beat control is the speed of the inner pulse.

## What the first screen is showing

The top of the Studio screen shows the current band, beat frequency, left and
right carrier frequencies, preset, visual mode, and session time. The colored
band ribbon is a quick label for the beat speed:

- Delta: very slow.
- Theta: slow and drifting.
- Alpha: calm and steady.
- Beta: quicker and more alert.
- Gamma: fast and bright.

Those names come from EEG vocabulary. In MicroTube they are useful labels, not
guarantees. Your state, volume, room, headphones, and expectations matter.

## A safe first pass

Start with the default Relaxation preset. Keep the volume low enough that you
could comfortably listen for half an hour. MicroTube is built around steady
tones, and steady tones can feel louder over time than they seem in the first
few seconds.

Try this:

- Press `v` a few times to cycle the visual modes.
- Press `j` and `k` to move between parameters.
- Use `h` and `l` for small changes.
- Press `t` to change the tone color.
- Press `n` to add or remove the mist layer.

You do not need to activate everything. A clean carrier, a little warmth, and a
small amount of mist can be more useful than a busy sound.

## What to listen for

At first, listen for three layers.

The carrier is the actual pitch in each ear. It is the stable tone underneath
everything else.

The beat is the perceived wobble between the ears. It may feel centered in the
head rather than coming from either side.

The texture is everything added around the carrier: harmonic warmth, mist,
emergence voices, and drift. These are optional layers. They exist to make the
sound less sterile and to give long sessions a sense of motion.

## What MicroTube is not

MicroTube is not a medical device and does not promise a mental state. It is an
audio instrument built from binaural beats, harmonic tone design, noise colors,
and a few generative systems. Treat the band names as a map legend, not as a
remote control for the brain.

The best way to use it is observational. Change one thing, listen for a minute,
then decide whether the change helped.
