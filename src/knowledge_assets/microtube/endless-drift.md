# Endless Drift

Drift is MicroTube's Shepard-Risset layer. It creates the illusion of a pitch
that keeps rising or keeps falling without ever arriving.

The trick is a stack of tones separated by octaves. As one tone moves out of
range, another fades in. Your ear follows the motion and misses the handoff.

## Why it is separate from the beat

The binaural beat depends on the difference between the left and right ears.
Drift is mixed equally into both ears, so it does not change that difference.

That means drift can add movement while the beat stays fixed.

## Direction

Use `r` to turn drift on or off. Use `R` to reverse direction.

Rising drift can feel like lift, effort, or acceleration. Falling drift can
feel like descent, settling, or gravity.

Those are impressions, not rules. The same motion can feel different depending
on beat speed, mist, and volume.

## Intensity

The Drift parameter controls how audible the layer is. At low intensity, it is
more like a current under the carrier. At high intensity, it becomes a main
feature.

Drift Base controls the bottom frequency of the seven-octave stack. Lower
values make the motion heavier and darker; higher values make it brighter and
more present.

If you want a stable meditation tone, keep drift low. If you want a cinematic
journey, let it rise.

## What to watch

The Envelope and Penrose views make drift feel more dramatic because they
already show motion. The Waveform view makes it easier to remember that the
carrier is still steady underneath.
