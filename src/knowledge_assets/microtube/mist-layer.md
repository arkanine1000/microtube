# Mist Layer

Mist is the noise layer under the carrier. It does not create the binaural
beat. It gives the beat an environment.

Pure tones can feel exposed, especially over long sessions. A small amount of
mist can soften the edges and make the sound less like a lab signal.

## Pink

Pink mist is the balanced middle texture. It has more low energy than white
noise and usually feels less hissy.

Use Pink when you want texture without calling attention to itself.

## White

White mist spreads energy evenly across the frequency range. It can sound like
air or static.

Use White sparingly. It can brighten the whole session quickly.

## Brown

Brown mist leans low and is the default texture. It can feel like distant surf,
engine room rumble, or a soft floor under the tone.

Use Brown when the carrier feels too high or too dry.

## Blue

Blue mist leans bright. It can add glass, edge, and sparkle.

Use Blue when the sound needs presence, but keep the level modest.

## Velvet

Velvet mist is sparse. Instead of constant noise, it produces small fading
impulses. It can feel like dust, tiny sparks, or distant crackle.

Use Velvet for sessions that should feel alive without becoming full.

## Amount matters

The Noise parameter sets how much mist is mixed in. A little usually goes a
long way. If you stop hearing the beat clearly, reduce the mist before changing
the beat frequency.
