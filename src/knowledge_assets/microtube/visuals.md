# Reading The Visuals

The visuals are not just screensavers. Each one chooses a different way to
summarize the sound.

No visualization shows everything. Use them like instruments on a dashboard.

## Waveform

Waveform shows the left and right audio traces. It is the most literal view.
Use it when you want to see whether the channels are moving similarly or
differently.

## Spectrum

Spectrum shows energy across the recent audio buffer. It is not a studio-grade
analyzer, but it gives a useful sense of density and brightness.

Use it when mist, warmth, or saw timbre make the tone feel crowded.

## Harmonics

Harmonics shows two things: the live stereo phase trace and the active H1-H6
partial stack. This view is about tone color. It answers the question: what is
the warmth control adding right now?

## Envelope

Envelope shows the beat pulse as a moving shape. It is useful when the beat is
too fast or too subtle to count by ear.

## Penrose

Penrose is the geometry view. It is meant to show the ordered-but-never-simple
side of MicroTube: spirals, fivefold structure, and the Fibonacci worm idea
that drives Penrose emergence.

## Emergence

Emergence shows the voice ecosystem. Each node is a voice. Lines mark simple
relationships. The bottom line reports mode, energy, voice count, generation,
and epoch.

If this view is empty, emergence is probably off or still fading in.

## Choosing a view

For sound design, use Waveform, Spectrum, and Harmonics. For beat listening,
use Envelope. For generative behavior, use Penrose and Emergence.
